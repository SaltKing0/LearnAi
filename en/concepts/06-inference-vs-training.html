06 · Inference vs. Training

Explain it to a 12-year-old Training is school: long, expensive, the model learns. Inference is the exam: what was learned gets applied to produce an answer. At school you practice a thousand times — in the exam you just want the right result, fast. That's why the two phases cost completely different amounts and run on different hardware.

The two phases compared

Training:  data + backprop + weight updates  →  expensive, slow, GPU, once
Inference: input → prediction (no updates)   →  cheap*, fast, GPU/CPU, always

During inference the weights are not changed anymore — the model only "thinks" forward (forward pass), exactly the autoregressive loop from Concept 01. That's why a single token can be generated relatively quickly.

Why training is so much more expensive

  • Backprop needs the complete graph in both directions + intermediate values → far more memory and compute per step.
  • Epochs: the same data is passed through thousands of times.
  • Model size scales both, but training grows ~linearly stronger with batch size and steps.

That's why only a few players (OpenAI, Meta, …) train huge models — and most of us only do inference (or light fine-tuning).

What "expensive" means in practice

  • Training: millions of $ for a frontier model; days/weeks on hundreds of GPUs. You practically never do this yourself — you take a pre-trained model.
  • Inference: paid per token (API) or per GPU-hour (self-hosted). This is where you save: fewer tokens, smaller models, quantization (Concept 04), caching.

Why this is central for you as a code user

  • API choice: you pay for inference. Model size, latency and cost are purely inference topics.
  • Self-hosting: inference can run on your own GPU/CPU (e.g. via Ollama, vLLM) — training hardly ever.
  • Quantization/distillation target inference costs.
  • Fine-tuning (Stage C) is a compromise: short, small training on top of a pre-trained model.
