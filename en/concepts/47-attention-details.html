47 · Attention Details

Explain it like I'm 12 Read the sentence: "The bird that sat on the roof flew ___". To know which word is missing ("away"), you look back — mainly at "bird", less at "roof". Attention does exactly that: every word asks a question (Query), every other word offers a match (Key), and the content gets picked up (Value). With multiple heads you have several readers at once: one watches grammar, one watches meaning, one watches distance.

Intuition

Concept 03 explains attention as "words looking at each other". Here we go one level deeper: why scale by √d_k? Why multiple heads? And what actually happens in cross-attention? The goal is a precise mental model, not the full math derivation.

What happens technically

Q/K/V, precisely

  • Every token embedding is projected through three learned matrices: Q = x·W_Q, K = x·W_K, V = x·W_V.
  • Query: "what am I looking for?" · Key: "what do I offer for matching?" · Value: "what information do I pass along if I get matched?"
  • Attention weights = Softmax(Q·Kᵀ / √d_k) · V. So: similarity between query and keys → weights → weighted sum of values.

Why scale by √d_k?

The dot product of two random d_k-dimensional vectors grows with d_k. At large d_k the scores become huge and softmax saturates: one weight goes to 1, all others to 0 → gradients die and learning collapses. Dividing by √d_k keeps the score variance around ~1 no matter how large d_k is.

Multi-Head Attention

Instead of one big attention, h small heads run in parallel (each d_head = d_model/h); their outputs are concatenated and projected again. Why? A single attention pattern would have to do everything at once; multiple heads can learn different relations (syntactic bindings, coreference, local vs. global patterns). Cost stays roughly the same — just distributed.

Positional Encoding

Attention is permutation-invariant by nature — "dog bites man" would look identical to "man bites dog". Position information must be injected:

  • Sinusoidal (original transformer) → fixed sine/cosine patterns per position, added to the embedding.
  • Learned position embeddings → a lookup table, but capped maximum length.
  • RoPE (Rotary Position Embedding) → rotates Q/K vectors by a position-dependent angle; relative distances then fall out of the dot product. Dominant today (Llama, Mistral, GPT families), extrapolates well via interpolation (e.g. context stretching).

Why it helps me

  • I understand why models can miss long-range dependencies: attention weights are soft estimates, not lookups.
  • Jargon from papers/config files (n_heads, n_kv_heads, rope_theta, FlashAttention flags) is no longer magic.
  • Why context stretching (32k → 128k) is mostly RoPE tricks — and why quality can still degrade.
  • Connects to concept 46: fewer KV heads (GQA/MQA) = smaller cache = faster inference.
