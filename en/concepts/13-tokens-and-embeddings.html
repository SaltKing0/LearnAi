13 · Tokens & Embeddings

Explain it to a 12-year-old Imagine you have a huge box of LEGO bricks. Each brick is a small piece of meaning. Some bricks are whole words ("cat"), some are parts of words ("un-", "-break-", "-able"). A token is just one of these LEGO bricks. And the model's job is to figure out which brick comes next.

What is a Token?

A token is the smallest unit the model works with. It's not always a word — it can be a part of a word, a punctuation mark, or even a space. The process of splitting text into tokens is called tokenization.

Tokenization Examples

TextTokensCount
Hello world["Hello", " world"]2
I don't know["I", " don", "'t", " know"]4
Unbreakable["Un", "break", "able"]3

Why not just use words?

There are millions of words in all languages. But there are far fewer meaningful word parts. By using subword tokens, models can handle any word — even ones they've never seen before.

What is an Embedding?

An embedding is a vector (list of numbers) that represents a token. Similar tokens have similar embeddings. "Cat" and "dog" are close together. "Cat" and "car" are far apart.

Why this matters to you

  • Token counting → API pricing is per token, not per word
  • Context limits → "8K context" means 8000 tokens, not 8000 words
  • Multilingual → Subword tokens handle all languages efficiently
