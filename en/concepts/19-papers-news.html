19 · Reading Papers & News

Explain it to a 12-year-old The AI world moves so fast that books are already old when they're printed. To keep up, you read the researchers' short notes ("papers") and see what's happening — but you don't have to understand every word, just the essentials.

Where the information is

  • arXiv (arxiv.org) — new research papers daily. cs.CL (language AI), cs.LG (machine learning), cs.AI.
  • Hugging Face blog / model cards (11) — practical, often with examples instead of formulas.
  • Lab blogs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta) — product + high-level explanation.
  • Newsletters / podcasts — curated summaries.

How to "read" a paper (without understanding everything)

1. Abstract + Conclusion  →  "What do they claim?"
2. Figures/Tables         →  "What do the results show?"
3. Related Work           →  "Where does it sit in the field?"
4. Method only if needed  →  the math is usually optional

Most intuition is in the abstract + figures. The deep math you only need if you want to rebuild it yourself (→ notebooks, 13).
