50 · Specialized Domains

Explain it to a 12-year-old Beyond "normal" web apps, programmers build in special worlds: tiny chips in your car (embedded), apps on your phone (mobile), giant data factories (data engineering), and machines that learn (AI). Each world has its own tools and rules — but the same core ideas (code, data, networking) underneath.

Intuition

This page is a map of the wider landscape — a tour of the specialized domains you'll hear about, so you can place them and decide where to dig deeper. The common thread: every domain is the same fundamentals (data structures, networking, concurrency) applied with domain-specific constraints.

Mobile matters for AI: on-device models (small LLMs), chat apps, and edge inference are mobile-domain problems.

Data engineering & analytics

  • ETL: Extract, Transform, Load — moving/cleaning data into a warehouse.
  • Apache Spark: distributed processing of huge datasets (map/reduce style).
  • Kafka: high-throughput event streaming — the backbone of event-driven systems (42) and real-time data.
  • Data warehousing: Snowflake, BigQuery — massive analytical SQL stores. (The data side that feeds model training and RAG corpora.)

Embedded systems & IoT

  • Low-level programming: C/C++/Rust close to the hardware, no OS or a tiny one.
  • Microcontrollers: small chips (Arduino, ESP32) with constrained memory and power.
  • RTOS (Real-Time Operating Systems): guarantees tasks finish within a deadline — used in cars, medical devices, industrial control.
  • IoT: networks of these devices talking to the cloud.

Why this helps me (as an AI/code user)

  • Perspective: knowing the whole landscape helps you see where your AI work fits and what to borrow (e.g. Kafka for agent event streams, on-device models for mobile).
  • Data for AI: data engineering builds the corpora and RAG pipelines models depend on.
  • Edge AI: running small models on phones/IoT is where embedded + ML meet (27, 31).
