10 · Fine-Tuning (LoRA / PEFT)

Explain it to a 12-year-old Imagine you have a chef who knows how to cook everything. Now you want them to specialize in pizza. Instead of teaching them to cook from scratch (that's pre-training), you just have them practice pizza for a week. That's fine-tuning — taking a generalist and making them a specialist.

What is Fine-Tuning?

Fine-tuning takes a pre-trained model and continues training on a smaller, specialized dataset. The model retains its general knowledge but becomes better at specific tasks.

When to Fine-Tune?

  • Domain expertise → Medical, legal, technical jargon
  • Style/voice → Match your brand's tone
  • Format → Always output JSON, specific templates
  • Behavior → How to handle edge cases

LoRA / PEFT

Full fine-tuning is expensive — you update all billions of parameters. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) only updates small "adapter" layers. Same result, 1% of the cost.

Fine-Tuning vs. RAG vs. Prompting

MethodCostWhen
PromptingFreeSimple tasks, quick iteration
RAGLowNeed external/up-to-date knowledge
Fine-TuningHighNeed specific behavior/style

Why this matters to you

  • Custom models → Build models that match your exact needs
  • Privacy → Keep sensitive data in-house
  • Cost → Smaller fine-tuned models can replace larger general ones
