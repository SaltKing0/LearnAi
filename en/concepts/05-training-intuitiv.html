Concept 05 · Stage B

05 · Training intuitively

Explain it to a 12-year-old Imagine you're throwing a ball at a target. After every throw someone tells you: "too high", "too short". You adjust your throw a tiny bit in the right direction. After a thousand throws you almost always hit. That's exactly how a model learns: it guesses, gets a "error note" (loss), and turns its numbers a little bit toward the better answer.

The three words that explain everything

  • Loss — how wrong the prediction was. High = bad, low = good. For a language model: "how surprised was the model by the real next word?" (technically: cross-entropy over the probabilities from Concept 01).
  • Backpropagation — the procedure that computes in which direction each of the millions of numbers (weights) must be turned so the loss goes down. You don't need the math — just know: it's the "error note propagated backwards through the network".
  • Epoch / Step — one pass over batches of data. The whole thing repeats thousands of times ("training").

The training loop (simplified)

for each epoch:
    batch      = take a bite of training data
    prediction = model(batch)
    loss       = error(prediction, true_answer)
    gradients  = backprop(loss)         # where to turn?
    weights   -= learning_rate * gradients  # a bit better

That's all. The learning rate = how big the "step" is per correction. Too large = overshoots; too small = takes forever.

What you really need to know as a code user

  • You usually don't train yourself — you use pre-trained models. But understanding what happens helps with fine-tuning (Stage C) and reading logs ("loss: 2.1 → 0.8" = it's learning).
  • Overfitting: the model memorizes training examples instead of the rule → performs badly on new data. Hence the validation set.
  • More epochs ≠ better: too many = overfitting. The "sweet spot" is read off the validation loss.
  • Fine-tuning (later) = the same loop, but only on a small, specific dataset and usually with a small learning rate.
