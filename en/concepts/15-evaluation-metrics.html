15 · Evaluation & Metrics

Explain it to a 12-year-old When you test whether someone can do math, you give them problems and count how many they solve correctly. With language models you do the same: a list of questions + reference answers, and you measure how often the answer fits.

Why evaluation is needed at all

"The model seems good" is not a measurement. As soon as you have to choose between models, prompts (07), or fine-tunes (10), you need a repeatable number. Otherwise you optimize by feel — and feel deceives at 1000 tokens of output.

The two most important metrics (intuitively)

  • Accuracy / F1 — for clearly gradeable tasks (classification, multiple choice): what percentage is correct. Simple, but needs unambiguous answers.
  • Perplexity (surprise) — how "surprised" the model was by the real next word (Concept 01/02). Lower = better predicted. Measures language-model quality without labels, just from the text itself.

Why this is central for you as a code user

  • Model selection: instead of "I like GPT-4o" → "on my task: 91% vs. 84%".
  • Spot regressions: after a prompt change, better or worse?
  • Know the benchmarks: MMLU, HellaSwag, GSM8K — public tasks on which models are compared (Stage F: reading papers).
